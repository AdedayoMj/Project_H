Produce the final variable-rate irrigation prescription for every field season listed in `/app/input/manifest.json`. The locally generated evidence represents projected field boundaries, campaign-specific analysis grids and crop-development curves, soil-map partitions with layered hydraulic properties, per-cell Sentinel-2 observations, daily weather, initial water and salinity states, spatial irrigation events with varying water quality, and campaign-wide pump budgets. `/app/input/specification.md` is the normative definition of the input schemas, geometry rules, coupled daily state transitions, constrained allocation, management zones, ordering, numeric semantics, and output schemas.

Write `/app/output/prescription.geojson`, `/app/output/units.csv`, and `/app/output/summary.json`. The GeoJSON must contain the prescribed clipped analysis-unit geometries and all properties in the normative schema. The CSV must contain the corresponding canonical per-unit records. The JSON summary must contain the required field and zone aggregates. Results must cover every listed campaign exactly once and use finite JSON numbers.

Unit identities, grid positions, integer stress counts, quota flags, and zone assignments are exact. Geometry and clipped area must reproduce the prescribed overlay to within $10^{-6}$ square metres, and area fractions to within $10^{-6}$. Final water state, requested/allocated prescription depths, and allocation shortfalls are accepted within the larger of $0.01$ mm or $0.1\%$ of the reference magnitude. Each accumulated seasonal depth and the accumulated salt-leaching index is accepted only within $0.01$ absolute. Crop coefficients, water/salinity stress coefficients, final EC, and allocation priorities are accepted within the larger of $0.0001$ or $0.1\%$. Volumes are accepted within the larger of $0.01$ cubic metres or $0.1\%$. These bands accommodate float64 overlay, summation-order, and serialization effects only; they do not admit coherent bias, intermediate rounding, a per-unit substitute for the joint allocation, or a different physical model or state-transition convention.
