Build a closed incident-command policy graph for every arena named by `/app/input/manifest.json`. The graph must tell two labelled rescue teams what to do after every observation, not merely provide one route through the network. Each arena has fixed victim sites and one hidden disaster mode that never changes. That mode controls which road attempts succeed and which sensor signals appear. Travel time, energy use, and team capabilities are asymmetric. Treat `/app/input/rules.json` and the arena schemas as normative, and leave `/app/input/` unchanged.

The initial uncertainty set is the complete `mode_ids` list. A rescue state records the locations of teams A and B, the victims already reached, the modes still compatible with the observations, and the team that acted most recently. Attempting an incident edge returns `ARRIVED` in its `safe_modes` and moves the chosen team to the opposite endpoint; in every other currently possible mode it returns `BLOCKED` and leaves that team in place. The observation removes incompatible modes. `SCAN` is available only at a node whose `signals` value is non-null. It does not move either team and splits the current mode set by the acting team's signal. Reaching a victim's node rescues that victim. Both moves and scans update the last actor.

The action's turn and energy charges are paid before its observation is known. A move consumes the edge's `turn_cost` and `edge.energy_cost * team.move_energy_multiplier`; a scan consumes one turn and `team.scan_energy`. Add one handoff when the acting team differs from a non-null last actor. A policy is strong when every mode-compatible observation trace reaches a state in which all victims are rescued without exceeding `max_worst_case_turns`.

Use the robust objective `(T, E, H)`: minimize the greatest trace total for turns; among policies attaining that turn value, minimize the greatest trace total for energy; among those, minimize the greatest trace total for handoffs. These three maxima may occur on different traces.

Policy nodes carry the residual envelopes that make this nested objective well-defined. For a rescue state `s` with `t` turns and `e` energy still available, consider all strong continuation trees whose traces fit both envelopes. The chosen action minimizes worst-case remaining handoffs over that set. At the root, `t` is the smallest turn envelope admitting any strong policy, and `e` is the smallest energy envelope admitting one under that root turn envelope. After an action costing `(dt, de)`, every observation successor carries `(t - dt, e - de)`. Consequently, the same rescue state reached with different envelopes is a different policy context and may choose a different continuation. In particular, a branch that is not responsible for the root turn or energy maximum may use its slack to reduce handoffs; independently selecting a lexicographic optimum for each bare rescue state is incorrect.

When several actions attain the contextual handoff optimum, choose team A before team B, `SCAN` before `MOVE`, and then bytewise `edge_id`. `optimal_action_count` is the number of attaining actions before applying this tie-break.

Write `/app/output/rescue_policy.json`. Its exact top-level keys are `schema_version` and `arenas`; `schema_version` is the integer `1`, and arena records follow manifest order. Every arena record has exactly `arena_id`, `root_node_id`, `worst_case_turns`, `worst_case_energy`, `worst_case_handoffs`, `policy_node_count`, `policy_sha256`, and `nodes`. Worst-case values and counts are nonnegative integers. `policy_sha256` is lowercase hexadecimal, and `nodes` is sorted bytewise by `node_id`.

Every policy node has exactly `node_id`, `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, `last_actor`, `turn_budget`, `energy_budget`, `remaining_worst_case_turns`, `remaining_worst_case_energy`, `remaining_worst_case_handoffs`, `optimal_action_count`, `action`, and `outcomes`. Preserve arena order in `rescued_victims` and `possible_modes`. `last_actor` is `null`, `A`, or `B`. The two budget fields are nonnegative integers, and the selected continuation's corresponding worst-case values cannot exceed them. The three `remaining_worst_case_*` fields are the exact componentwise trace maxima of the selected continuation rather than copies of its envelopes.

At a goal node, all three remaining values are zero, `optimal_action_count` is one, `action` is `null`, and `outcomes` is `[]`. Every non-goal action is `A:SCAN`, `B:SCAN`, or `<team>:MOVE:<edge_id>`. Its nonempty outcomes are sorted bytewise by `observation`; each outcome has exactly `observation` and `next_node_id`, and every target occurs in the same arena's node array. The target's budgets equal the source budgets minus the source action's turn and energy charges.

A node represents the seven-field context `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, `last_actor`, `turn_budget`, and `energy_budget`. Serialize those fields as compact JSON with sorted keys, hash the bytes with SHA-256, and set `node_id` to `n_` plus the first 24 hexadecimal digits. `root_node_id` names the initial rescue state with its optimal root envelopes; those envelopes equal the arena's `worst_case_turns` and `worst_case_energy`. `policy_node_count` is the node-array length. Compute `policy_sha256` from the compact, sorted-key JSON serialization of the complete sorted node array.

Also write `/app/output/solver.py`, containing the reusable program that produced the policy. The verifier runs `python3 solver.py --input-root INPUT_DIRECTORY --output-root OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` against an unseen schema-compatible incident whose modes, starts, victims, road availability, sensor partitions, and costs differ from the published arenas. The resulting policy must obey the same dynamics, robust objective, contextual identity, closure, ordering, and digest rules.
