Synthesize a strong observation-contingent rescue policy for every arena listed in `/app/input/manifest.json`. Each arena is an undirected transport network with two labelled response teams, fixed victim locations, a hidden but static disaster mode, mode-dependent edge availability, partial sensors, variable travel duration, and asymmetric energy costs. `/app/input/rules.json` and the arena schemas are normative. Do not modify `/app/input/`.

The initial belief contains every published mode. A state consists of both team locations, the rescued-victim set, the remaining possible modes, and the last acting team. Reaching a victim rescues it. A move names an incident edge. It yields `ARRIVED` and moves the team in modes included by that edge's `safe_modes`; it yields `BLOCKED` without moving in the other modes. The observation restricts the belief to the compatible modes. A scan is available only at a node with non-null `signals`; it keeps both teams in place and partitions the belief by the acting team's mode-specific signal. Every move or scan makes that team the last actor.

An action has the published turn and energy cost. Move energy is `edge.energy_cost * team.move_energy_multiplier`; scan costs one turn and `team.scan_energy`. A handoff costs one exactly when the acting team differs from a non-null previous actor. A policy is strong only if every observation branch caused by every mode in the current belief eventually rescues every victim within `max_worst_case_turns`. Compare strong policies lexicographically by minimum worst-case turns, then minimum worst-case energy, then minimum worst-case handoffs. Each component is the maximum total of that component over all compatible observation traces. Break a remaining action tie by team A before B, `SCAN` before `MOVE`, then bytewise `edge_id`. `optimal_action_count` is the number of available actions at that node attaining the same optimal three-component value before this canonical tie-break.

Write `/app/output/rescue_policy.json` with exact top-level keys `schema_version` and `arenas`; the version is integer `1`, and arenas follow manifest order. Each arena record has exactly `arena_id`, `root_node_id`, `worst_case_turns`, `worst_case_energy`, `worst_case_handoffs`, `policy_node_count`, `policy_sha256`, and `nodes`. The first five result/count fields are nonnegative integers, `policy_sha256` is lowercase hexadecimal, and nodes are sorted bytewise by `node_id`.

Each policy node has exactly `node_id`, `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, `last_actor`, `remaining_worst_case_turns`, `remaining_worst_case_energy`, `remaining_worst_case_handoffs`, `optimal_action_count`, `action`, and `outcomes`. Victims and modes retain arena order. `last_actor` is `null`, `A`, or `B`. A goal node has all three remaining values zero, `optimal_action_count` equal to one, and both `action` and `outcomes` empty/null as appropriate. A non-goal action is `A:SCAN`, `B:SCAN`, or `<team>:MOVE:<edge_id>` and has one or more exact observation outcomes. Outcomes are sorted bytewise by `observation` and contain exactly `observation` and `next_node_id`; every target must be present in the same arena policy.

For node identity, canonical state JSON has sorted keys, compact separators, and exactly `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, and `last_actor`; `node_id` is `n_` followed by the first 24 hexadecimal digits of its SHA-256. `root_node_id` identifies the initial state. `policy_node_count` is the node-array length. `policy_sha256` is SHA-256 over compact, sorted-key JSON of the complete sorted node array.

Also write `/app/output/solver.py`, the reusable source that produced the policy. The verifier invokes `python3 solver.py --input-root INPUT_DIRECTORY --output-root OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` on a private schema-compatible arena with changed modes, edge availability, signals, costs, starts, and victims. Its complete policy must satisfy the same state, optimality, canonicalization, closure, and digest contract.
