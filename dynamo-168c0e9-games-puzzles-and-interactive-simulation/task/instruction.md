Synthesize a strong observation-contingent rescue policy for every arena listed in `/app/input/manifest.json`. Each arena is an undirected transport network with two labelled response teams, fixed victim locations, a hidden but static disaster mode, mode-dependent edge availability, partial sensors, variable travel duration, and asymmetric energy costs. `/app/input/rules.json` and the arena schemas are normative. Do not modify `/app/input/`.

The initial belief contains every published mode. A state consists of both team locations, the rescued-victim set, the remaining possible modes, and the last acting team. Reaching a victim rescues it. A move names an incident edge. It yields `ARRIVED` and moves the team in modes included by that edge's `safe_modes`; it yields `BLOCKED` without moving in the other modes. The observation restricts the belief to the compatible modes. A scan is available only at a node with non-null `signals`; it keeps both teams in place and partitions the belief by the acting team's mode-specific signal. Every move or scan makes that team the last actor.

An action has the published turn and energy cost. Move energy is `edge.energy_cost * team.move_energy_multiplier`; scan costs one turn and `team.scan_energy`. A handoff costs one exactly when the acting team differs from a non-null previous actor. A policy is strong only if every observation branch caused by every mode in the current belief eventually rescues every victim within `max_worst_case_turns`. Compare strong policies lexicographically by minimum worst-case turns, then minimum worst-case energy, then minimum worst-case handoffs. Each component is the maximum total of that component over all compatible observation traces.

Perform that lexicographic minimax without independently fixing a lexicographically optimal continuation for each observation branch. First find the minimum feasible root turn budget. Under that turn cap, find the minimum feasible root energy budget. Under both caps, minimize worst-case handoffs. An observation child inherits the parent turn and energy budgets minus the chosen action's costs; within that inherited context it minimizes handoffs subject to both caps. Thus a branch below the worst-case turn or energy trace may deliberately spend its slack to improve a later objective. Break an action tie by team A before B, `SCAN` before `MOVE`, then bytewise `edge_id`. At a policy node, `optimal_action_count` is the number of available actions attaining the minimum worst-case handoffs within that node's published turn and energy budgets before this canonical tie-break.

Write `/app/output/rescue_policy.json` with exact top-level keys `schema_version` and `arenas`; the version is integer `1`, and arenas follow manifest order. Each arena record has exactly `arena_id`, `root_node_id`, `worst_case_turns`, `worst_case_energy`, `worst_case_handoffs`, `policy_node_count`, `policy_sha256`, and `nodes`. All worst-case and count fields are nonnegative integers, `policy_sha256` is lowercase hexadecimal, and nodes are sorted bytewise by `node_id`.

Each policy node has exactly `node_id`, `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, `last_actor`, `turn_budget`, `energy_budget`, `remaining_worst_case_turns`, `remaining_worst_case_energy`, `remaining_worst_case_handoffs`, `optimal_action_count`, `action`, and `outcomes`. Victims and modes retain arena order. `last_actor` is `null`, `A`, or `B`. The two budgets are nonnegative integers; the corresponding selected-policy worst-case values must not exceed them. Every non-goal outcome target has the parent budgets minus the action's turn and energy costs. A goal node has all three remaining values zero, `optimal_action_count` equal to one, `action` equal to `null`, and `outcomes` equal to the empty array `[]`. A non-goal action is `A:SCAN`, `B:SCAN`, or `<team>:MOVE:<edge_id>` and has one or more exact observation outcomes. Outcomes are sorted bytewise by `observation` and contain exactly `observation` and `next_node_id`; every target must be present in the same arena policy.

For node identity, canonical context JSON has sorted keys, compact separators, and exactly `team_a_node`, `team_b_node`, `rescued_victims`, `possible_modes`, `last_actor`, `turn_budget`, and `energy_budget`; `node_id` is `n_` followed by the first 24 hexadecimal digits of its SHA-256. `root_node_id` identifies the initial state paired with the minimum feasible root turn and energy budgets. At the root, those budgets equal `worst_case_turns` and `worst_case_energy`. `remaining_worst_case_turns`, `remaining_worst_case_energy`, and `remaining_worst_case_handoffs` are the exact componentwise maxima of the selected contextual policy, not merely its caps. `policy_node_count` is the node-array length. `policy_sha256` is SHA-256 over compact, sorted-key JSON of the complete sorted node array.

Also write `/app/output/solver.py`, the reusable source that produced the policy. The verifier invokes `python3 solver.py --input-root INPUT_DIRECTORY --output-root OUTPUT_DIRECTORY` on a private schema-compatible arena with changed modes, edge availability, signals, costs, starts, and victims. Its complete policy must satisfy the same state, optimality, canonicalization, closure, and digest contract.
